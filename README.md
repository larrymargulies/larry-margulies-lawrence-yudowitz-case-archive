# Larry Margulies / Lawrence Yudowitz — Public Investigative Case Records Archive

This public archive provides an organized investigative index to source records concerning Larry Margulies / Lawrence Yudowitz. It is intended to facilitate genuine investigative review by citizens and, where relevant, law-enforcement personnel, professional investigators, attorneys, journalists, researchers, oversight bodies, and other legitimate reviewers.

The archive currently preserves **15 source records**.

## Purpose — Investigative Source-Record Repository

This repository is maintained as a neutral, evidence-first public investigative case-record archive.

Its purpose is to preserve and organize primary source materials so reviewers can locate records efficiently, establish chronology, trace document provenance, compare information across records and jurisdictions, identify corroborating or conflicting information, and identify evidentiary gaps requiring further investigation.

It is not presented as a commercial brand, course, capstone, podcast, promotional project, or entertainment collection.

## Investigator / Law-Enforcement Review

For efficient initial review, materials should generally be examined in the following order:

1. Complaints and supplemental complaints
2. Affidavits and sworn statements
3. Official correspondence and agency records
4. Exhibits and supporting source records
5. Publicly releasable witness/source material
6. Chronology and cross-record relationships
7. Records-request activity and institutional responses
8. Preservation and integrity information
9. Unresolved investigative questions and evidentiary gaps

Where supported by the underlying records, indexed items should identify the document date, originating source, jurisdiction, document type, complaint/case/report or exhibit number, provenance, version status, related persons/events/records, preservation location, and integrity information.

## Evidentiary Status

**DOCUMENTED FACT** — information directly established by an identified source record.

**ATTRIBUTED STATEMENT** — information stated by an identified person, agency, institution, document, or other source.

**ALLEGATION** — a claim contained in a complaint, affidavit, correspondence, statement, or other record that is not established as fact merely because it has been documented or published.

**CORROBORATING MATERIAL** — a separate identified source supporting some aspect of another record or statement. Corroboration of one fact does not establish every associated allegation.

**CONFLICTING / CONTRADICTORY MATERIAL** — identified records or statements that materially differ and may require reconciliation or further investigation.

**INFERENCE / ANALYTICAL NOTE** — an interpretation, proposed relationship, hypothesis, or analytical connection derived from identified source materials rather than a fact directly established by those materials.

**UNRESOLVED / MISSING INFORMATION** — information requiring additional records, witness interviews, identification, authentication, agency confirmation, or other independent investigative work.

## Preservation, Provenance and Integrity

Original source documents are preserved without rewriting their substantive contents. Historical filenames may be retained where necessary for provenance.

Any annotation, correction, transcription, redaction, conversion, summary, or other derivative should be separately identified rather than silently replacing an underlying source record.

Where available, SHA-256 checksums, persistent repository identifiers, publication/acquisition dates, and version history should be maintained to assist integrity review.

## Independent Verification

Reviewers should independently verify material facts through original records, originating agencies, witnesses, custodians, courts, service providers, or other appropriate sources wherever practicable.

Potential leads, relationships, contradictions, missing records, and witness information are presented to facilitate further inquiry, not to predetermine its result.

Publication does not independently authenticate a document, establish the truth of an allegation, establish guilt or probable cause, determine admissibility, require investigative or prosecutorial action, constitute a judicial finding, or represent endorsement by any law-enforcement agency, court, government body, or other institution.
